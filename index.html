About me

I am currently a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Department of Materials Science and NanoEngineering at Rice University.

Materials performance and lifetime are governed by atomic-scale mechanisms, often under conditions difficult to probe experimentally, such as extreme temperatures and stresses. My research focuses on understanding how atomic structure, bonding, and defects influence properties such as mechanical strength, heat transport, and degradation resistance. My recent research has focused on machine learning methods, particularly equivariant neural networks and deep learning interatomic potentials, to explore these mechanisms at larger scales.

Research

Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials

Machine learning interatomic potentials carry first-principles accuracy into the long molecular-dynamics trajectories required for transport calculations. We train a DeepMD potential to reproduce the energetics and forces of point defects in zirconium carbide (ZrC), enabling long-timescale simulations of defect-controlled thermal conductivity through the Green–Kubo formalism at high temperatures.

  • Defect-Controlled Thermal Conductivity of ZrC at High Temperatures from First Principles — In Preparation (2026).

Thermal transport figure placeholder

Thermal Transport

We combine linear response theory with equilibrium and non-equilibrium molecular dynamics to investigate thermal transport in regimes governed by strong anharmonic effects, including ultrahigh temperatures and structures with dynamical defects. These approaches connect heat-current fluctuations and imposed temperature gradients to thermal conductivity over the long trajectories needed to resolve transport accurately.

  • Defect-Controlled Thermal Conductivity of ZrC at High Temperatures from First Principles — In Preparation (2026).
  • Kapitza Resistance and Thermal Transport in Knotted Graphene Nanoribbons — Applied Surface Science 742, 167187 (2026).

Atomistic Materials Degradation

This research connects quantum chemical calculations and transition state theory to identify rate-limiting steps in chemical degradation processes, including water-driven stress corrosion cracking and the thermal decomposition and sublimation of multicomponent perchlorate crystals. First-principles and density functional tight-binding calculations, combined with metadynamics, are used to resolve these rare events at the atomic scale.

  • Topochemistry of Ammonium Perchlorate: How Surface Morphology Mediates Its Sublimation — The Journal of Physical Chemistry Letters 17(31), 9031–9037 (2026).
  • Ab Initio Molecular Dynamics Insights into Stress Corrosion Cracking and Dissolution of Metal Oxides — Materials 18(3), 538 (2025).

Nanomechanics

My earlier research focused on the mechanical response of carbon nanomaterials and architected carbon structures. These studies combined density functional theory, molecular dynamics, and mechanical modeling to understand deformation, fracture, thermal stability, impact response, and energy absorption from the atomistic to the macroscale.

  • Mechanical and Energy-Absorption Properties of Schwarzites — Carbon 157, 670–680 (2020).
  • Mechanical Properties of Diamond Schwarzites: From Atomistic Models to 3D-Printed Structures — MRS Advances 5(33–34), 1775–1781 (2020).
  • On the Mechanical Properties and Thermal Stability of a Recently Synthesized Monolayer Amorphous Carbon — The Journal of Physical Chemistry C 124(27), 14855–14860 (2020).
  • Guided Fractures in Graphene Mechanical Diode-Like Structures — Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics 24(22), 13905–13910 (2022).
  • Mechanical Response of Pentadiamond: A DFT and Molecular Dynamics Study — Physica B: Condensed Matter 629, 413576 (2022).
  • Mechanical Properties of 3D-Printed Pentadiamond — Journal of Physics D: Applied Physics 55(46), 465301 (2022).
